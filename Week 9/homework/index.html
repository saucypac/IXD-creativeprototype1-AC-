P5.JS / OBJECT-ORIENTED PROGRAMMING

Living Ecosystem

An interactive underwater environment exploring how individual objects influence one another through simple rules and relationships.

01 / Project Intent

For this project, I wanted to create an underwater ecosystem where different organisms could interact with one another. Rather than having everything follow a fixed animation, I wanted each object to respond to what was happening around it.

Fish search for food, avoid predators, and move alongside other fish. Predators chase nearby fish, while food provides a reason for fish to gather. These simple relationships create more complex behaviors throughout the environment.

05 / Development Process

I developed the project in five stages, gradually introducing new elements and interactions into the underwater environment.

Stage 1 — Underwater Environment

I started by creating the underwater setting using basic shapes and animation. I added light rays, rising bubbles, and swaying seaweed to make the environment feel alive.

Stage 2 — Fish Objects

I created a Fish class using Object-Oriented Programming. Each fish has its own position, velocity, size, and color, allowing multiple fish to move independently throughout the environment.

Stage 3 — Food Relationships

I introduced a Food class and programmed fish to detect nearby food. When food is within range, fish gradually change direction toward it and consume it when close enough. Eating food also restores their energy.

Stage 4 — Predators and Emergent Behavior

I added predators that search for and chase nearby fish. Fish respond by swimming away from danger. I also introduced schooling and separation behaviors, allowing fish to move together while avoiding crowding.

Stage 5 — User Interaction

I added mouse and keyboard controls so users can directly influence the ecosystem. Clicking adds food, while keyboard inputs introduce new fish or predators and reset the environment.

06 / Emergent Behavior

One of the most interesting parts of this project is how the fish behave as a group. Each fish follows a relatively simple set of rules, but their interactions create larger movement patterns.

Fish may gather around food, scatter when predators approach, and regroup afterward. These behaviors are not controlled by a predetermined animation. Instead, they emerge from the relationships between individual objects.

07 / Reflection

This project helped me understand how Object-Oriented Programming can be used to create interactive systems. By separating organisms into classes, I was able to give each object its own properties and behaviors while allowing them to interact.

The most interesting challenge was combining multiple behaviors without making the movement feel too mechanical. Small changes to the environment, such as introducing additional food or predators, could affect how the entire ecosystem behaved.
