Design Reading Reflections

GIXD-503A | Interaction Design

Reading 1: Programming Design Systems

Quote 1

"…static design tools like illustrator and sketch fail at prototyping digital systems.” p3

This is really interesting to mention, as I remembered when I was in highschool, one of my assignments was to create a website based on a specific product that I was tasked with. I remember struggling a lot with illustrators as I had a lot of different screens created to show my thought process. It was really tiring in the end, and I wondered back then if there were better tools that can illustrate my thought process of prototyping my ideas out loud instead of speaking it.

Quote 2

“Is google.com a good website because of the look of the search field?” p3

Google is not a good website because of how the search field looks. Sure it is clear where we should type, and visually it's appealing as well, but it's the functionality and integration that makes it a good website. Google’s search bar design hasn't really had big changes over the last 20ish years and that's because behind that page, there are complex systems that involve user trust and can be tailored specifically to the user based on what they type. In the end, it fits with the user and it allows individuals to find exactly what they are looking for without much work.

Quote 3

“Throwing a design over the wall for production is a bad legacy of the printed page, and there is no reason for the design process to end with the birth of a product.” p4

I heavily agree with this statement, just because you hand off a design to devs to implement or when the product is created, it doesn’t mean that we should abandon our work without any collaboration or communication. In the past, when creating physical prints, once the designer hands off their design to the printing press, the designers would have finished everything, but this mindset is incorrect as within the digital field, designers and developers need to work hand in hand with each other especially when shipping to shipped products as the designs are dynamic and continuously changing.

Reading 2: What do Prototypes Prototype?

Quote 1

“Tools can be used in many different ways, and detail is not a sure indicator of completeness.” (pg1)

This is an interesting line, as a super realistic prototype might look like a completed product, but internally it can be not working. On the other hand having a rough sketch might be able able to answer important questions early on and more effectively before spending large amounts of time and money to fabricate a prototype with all features added.

Quote 2

“Selecting the focus of a prototype is the art of identifying the most important open design questions” (pg1)

Prototyping isn’t about creating a complete product at the spot, but its about answering specific pressing design questions. So instead of spending all the time perfecting every detail on an interface or product, designers should focus on the areas with the most uncertainties within their idea. This makes prototyping more purposeful and can help avoid wasting time on things that might not matter as much.

Quote 3

“We define prototype as any representation of a design idea, regardless of medium”(pg 3)

At the end of the day, a prototype does not have to be a working product showcase. It can be a sketch, a physical model, or even a digital animation. All that matters is whether it helps communicate and test a specific idea.
